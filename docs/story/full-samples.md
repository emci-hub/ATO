# ATO Full Story Samples (v1, follows ato-story-writer.md + ato-story-rulebook.md)

## Swipe-card map
| Card | Beat | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | hook | 📍 setting line + setup + ticking clock |
| 2 | twist | turn + 2–3 dialogue lines + **DARK** joke |
| 3 | move | trait-shaped action (category + lean), rule of three, **LIGHT** joke |
| 4 | punchline | setups paid off + callback, funny word last |
| 5 | next + reveal | "Next:" teaser, then the italic reveal (≤15 words) |
| 6 | why this is you | the real category label + lean, the proof from this story, one kind tip (≤45 words) |
| tap | otherWay | optional hidden card: "The other way it could have gone" |

Joke markers like [DARK] / [LIGHT] are data tags and are never shown to users.

## Story 1 · Wrong group chat · cat_communication / friends / high
**Title: The Group Chat Whisperer**

— card 1 —
📍 *Couch. 9:14 PM. Wednesday.*
Your friend Riley is planning an escape-room birthday.
You rant about the $60 ticket price.
You hit send, then see the chat name: "Riley's Birthday 🎉."

— card 2 —
Three dots appear under Riley's name.
**Riley:** "…wait, who is this about?"
Your friend Jordan texts you privately.
**Jordan:** "Wrong chat. WRONG. CHAT."
Read receipts appear one by one, like a tiny firing squad. [DARK]

— card 3 —
You don't delete it.
You send: "That was a test of this chat's emergency system."
Then: "Real message: I'm in. I'm bringing a $60-bill cake." [LIGHT]

— card 4 —
Riley drops the ticket price to $40.
Then Riley renames the chat "Riley's Birthday (Budget Edition)."

— card 5 —
Next: a voice note you didn't mean to send.

*You can't unsend words. You can always send better ones.*

— card 6 · Why this is you —
This is your Communication: you say the hard thing, and keep it kind.
You scored {traitScore}.
Proof: you rewrote the chat instead of deleting it.
Watch out: not every slip needs a speech. "My bad" is plenty.

— tap: the other way —
*The other way, you'd have deleted it and claimed your phone was stolen.*

## Story 2 · First date, wrong person · cat_social / friends / high
**Title: Captain Table for Three**

— card 1 —
📍 *Café. 7:02 PM. Friday.*
You're meeting your date for the first time.
Someone by the window looks like your date's photos.
You wave, and they wave back.

— card 2 —
They sit down at your table.
This is Rory, who also has a first date tonight.
**Your date (standing behind you):** "Hi… I'm over here."
**Rory:** "Oh no. I thought you were my date."
Somewhere, your dignity quietly packs a small suitcase. [DARK]

— card 3 —
You pull up a third chair.
You introduce your date and Rory like you planned it.
Then you launch a search party for Rory's date, with croissants. [LIGHT]

— card 4 —
Rory's real date walks in at 7:10.
This time, everyone waits for them to wave first.

— card 5 —
Next: a party where you only know the host.

*Awkward moments don't scare you. You give them a chair.*

— card 6 · Why this is you —
This is your Everyday social energy: people charge you up, even strangers.
Proof: you turned a wrong-table mix-up into a table for three.
Tip: let the quiet person at the table pick the next topic. They'll remember it.

— tap: the other way —
*The other way, you'd have hidden behind the menu until Rory left.*

## Story 3a · Surprise guest in 15 minutes · cat_drive / home / high
**Title: Captain Fifteen Minutes**

— card 1 —
📍 *Apartment. 6:45 PM. Sunday.*
Your aunt Rosa texts: "In the neighbourhood! There in fifteen minutes."
Your apartment looks like a laundry tornado.

— card 2 —
Your sibling Blair stares at the chair of clothes.
**Blair:** "She said fifteen minutes. That means ten."
**Blair:** "And she always checks the fridge."
The clothes chair has grown for weeks, and now it stares back. [DARK]

— card 3 —
You set a timer and assign jobs.
Blair folds the clothes, and you speed-wash the dishes.
You put one lemon in the empty fridge, for elegance. [LIGHT]

— card 4 —
Aunt Rosa arrives at 6:55.
She opens the fridge, sees the lemon, and nods.
The clothes chair sits empty, plotting its comeback.

— card 5 —
Next: Aunt Rosa says she'll "pop by more often."

*You don't panic at deadlines. You hand out jobs.*

— card 6 · Why this is you —
This is your Drive: you push until it's done.
Proof: one timer, two jobs, and a single lemon.
Watch out: not every visit is a race. Some mess can stay. Aunt Rosa has seen worse.

— tap: the other way —
*The other way, you'd have shoved everything into one closet and prayed.*

## Story 3b · Surprise guest in 15 minutes · cat_drive / home / low
**Title: Professor Closet Door**

— card 1 —
📍 *Apartment. 6:45 PM. Sunday.*
Your aunt Rosa texts: "In the neighbourhood! There in fifteen minutes."
Your apartment looks like a laundry tornado.

— card 2 —
Your sibling Blair stares at the chair of clothes.
**Blair:** "She said fifteen minutes. That means ten."
**Blair:** "And she always checks the fridge."
The clothes chair has grown for weeks, and now it stares back. [DARK]

— card 3 —
You shove the clothes chair into the closet in one armful.
You lean on the door until it clicks, like a villain sealing a vault. [LIGHT]

— card 4 —
Aunt Rosa arrives at 6:55.
She opens the fridge, finds only ketchup, and says, "Very minimalist."
She reaches for the closet to hang her coat. [DARK sting]

— card 5 —
Next: Aunt Rosa invites herself to Sunday dinner.

*Your plans are messy, yet you always beat the clock.*

— card 6 · Why this is you —
This is your Drive: you go at your own pace and still get there.
Proof: one armful, one closet, zero cleaning.
Watch out: the closet remembers. Empty it before the next surprise visit.

— tap: the other way —
*The other way, you'd have set a timer, assigned jobs, and bought a lemon.*

## Self-critique (funny / captivating / makes sense, 1–10)
| Story | First draft | Problem | Final |

| 2 Wrong date | 7/8/8 | The punchline didn't call back the wave. The dark line "the café goes quiet" was too soft. | 8/8/9: "everyone waits for them to wave first"; dignity packs a suitcase |
| 3a Guest, high | 8/8/9 | The clothes chair vanished with no payoff | 8/8/9: "sits empty, plotting its comeback" |
| 3b Guest, low | 7/8/8 | Ended on "clothes get revenge" (said the joke out loud) | 9/8/9: ends as she *reaches* for the closet; the reader finishes the joke. The move was cut to 2 lines for the 120-word cap. |

### Rule checks (all pass)
- **Setups paid off:** fridge check → lemon / ketchup; the clothes chair; the guest arrives and reacts; the wave → waiting for them to wave first; the ticket price → Budget Edition.
- **People:** every speaker is introduced first: Riley and Jordan, your date ("standing behind you") and Rory (introduces themselves), Blair.
- **Length:** all ≤120 words (118 / 120 / 118 / ~120).
- **Dialogue:** 2–3 lines per story, each ≤12 words. Every reveal is ≤15 words.
- **Titles** pass "you're such a ___": Group Chat Whisperer, Captain Table for Three, Captain Fifteen Minutes, Professor Closet Door.
- **otherWay:** the 3a and 3b other-way lines describe each other's lean, a deliberate mirror.
